Compass Sans Font Issues

Compass Sans and Compass Serif are limiting how far we can scale theming across brands, because of how they align inside the font bounding box. To find the cause, we benchmarked them against high-performance fonts.

Inter is the reference: robust and consistent on every platform, typically utilized by highly scalable products.

Set with our typographic tokens, Inter and Compass Sans produce bounding boxes of exactly the same height at every size.

The problem sits inside that box. Compass Sans puts its baseline 2.04px higher than Inter does (using 16px as the reference size). The difference is small, but enough to look like a rendering issue, and it has been leaking into component padding for a long time.

Same box, different baseline

Both set at 16px with a 150% line height, so both boxes are exactly 24px tall. The blue rule is the baseline; the dashed rule is the vertical centre of the box.

What it forced the components to do

To make a 48px button look optically centred, the design library pads it 13px on top and 11px underneath. The yellow rule marks the centre of the cap height; the dashed rule marks the centre of the button. When they do not meet, the label looks off.

centre of cap height centre of the button

Why it matters

It breaks on every theme swap

The 13/11 split is tuned to one font's metrics. Point the same component at a different family — Nunito for Cartus, or the system faces behind Boreal and Material Design 3 — and the compensation becomes the error, pushing the label 1px low instead.

The workaround was never exact

Optically centring Compass Sans in a 48px button needs 13.82px on top and 10.18px underneath. Integer padding cannot express that, so every button ships about 0.82px high even with the fix in place.

Fixing the font fixes it everywhere

Correct the metrics once and padding goes back to symmetric on every platform at once. No per-font component variants, no per-brand overrides.

Nothing resizes

Only the ascender and descender move, and they move by equal and opposite amounts. The content box stays exactly 1.210em, so line-height tokens, component heights and every existing layout hold their current dimensions.

Where this lands

  • Cap height matches Inter to 0.03px. Capitals, headings, buttons and nav labels line up without per-component adjustment, which is where the size mismatch was most visible.
  • It is the cheapest scaled option. Text runs 3.6% wider than production, against 10.7% for the x-height match. Dense tables and narrow breakpoints survive it.
  • It closes about a third of the x-height gap as a by-product, without stretching the capitals past Inter.
  • The accepted trade is lowercase. Body copy still reads roughly 6% smaller than Inter. That is a drawing difference and no scale factor fixes it.

Before this goes anywhere

Compass Sans and Compass Serif are licensed from Colophon Foundry. Foundry agreements do not automatically grant modification or redistribution rights, and these builds are modified. Clear it before the files leave a local machine.

Compass Display carries separate provenance and needs checking on its own terms.

x-height refers to lowercase height. What matches Inter in the Cap build is cap height. Worth keeping the two terms apart in review.
